OpenAI countersues Apple over trade secrets and supplier pressure

OpenAI filed counterclaims against Apple on Friday in a San Jose court, according to Bloomberg and The Next Web. It accuses Apple of pressuring suppliers not to work on OpenAI's devices and of using broad employment agreements to keep engineers from joining OpenAI. The Financial Times reports that OpenAI also alleges a former employee who returned to Apple passed along product plans and supplier details. Apple sued OpenAI in July over alleged theft of hardware trade secrets, and Apple has said it found no evidence for OpenAI's claims.

Sources: The Decoder, Engadget, The Next Web, Financial Times

Other

Nvidia board reinstates Jensen Huang 11 days after accepting his resignation

Nvidia's board voted unanimously on November 23 to bring back Jensen Huang, Fortune reports. The board accepted his resignation on November 12, and CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO. The board said it had received input from senior levels of the US government, and Trump told CNBC he made a personal assurance, according to Fortune. The board's independent investigation remains open, and Fortune reports the board has since created a national security committee and is hiring a chief compliance officer.

Sources: SiliconANGLE AI, Fortune, The Verge, The Washington Post

Banned Nvidia Blackwell chips turn up at Shanghai data center, Bloomberg reports

Bloomberg reports that filings with Chinese regulators show two companies installed Nvidia Blackwell servers at a Shanghai data center linked to Alice Huang. One company, Shanghai Shuoyao, offered a GB300 NVL72 rack as collateral for a 96 million yuan ($14 million) loan. Bloomberg traced the rack to Taiwan's Gigabyte through a public warranty database; Gigabyte declined to say who first bought it. Bloomberg says it has no evidence that Megaspeed, the Singapore firm Alice Huang founded, directly diverted the chips, and Megaspeed denies wrongdoing.

Sources: The Decoder, Engadget, Bloomberg, Gizmodo

Papa John's order is first UK purchase paid by AI agent without a card

An order of nine mini pizzas became the first UK purchase made by an AI agent using cardless payment, The Guardian reports. The test ran in the Financial Conduct Authority's regulated sandbox with Lloyds Bank and payment firm TrueLayer. The agent worked under a mandate with a maximum spend per transaction and per month, and no account security details were shared. TrueLayer says it was the first time an AI agent paid a UK retailer for a real customer's order.

Sources: Finextra, PYMNTS, The Guardian AI, tech.eu

Poland signs $300 million, five-year AI deal with Palantir for armed forces

Poland signed a five-year deal worth around $300 million with Palantir on Friday, Politico reports. The deal expands the use of AI across Poland's armed forces and adopts Palantir's Maven system, which Ukraine and NATO already use. Defense Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz said it would be "stupid and wrong" not to use it.

Sources: Rapporteur (ex-Euractiv), Politico Europe Tech, defence24.com, ForkLog

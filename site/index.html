Google to cut Search access for schools that block its AI

Google announced new AI tools for classrooms and said schools can turn off its AI features. Google's education vice president, Chris Phillips, told the New York Times that Search and AI are integrated, so schools that ban AI will lose Search access for students. Google also said access to Gemini and Search AI features will be off for K-12 student accounts until administrators turn them on. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani and former FTC Chair Lina Khan accused Google of using its search monopoly to push AI into schools.

Why it matters: Schools that restrict Google's AI would also lose Search for students, which gives them a strong reason to opt in.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, Ars Technica AI, The Guardian AI, CNBC Technology

Policy & Regulation

Voters approve dozens of data center restrictions in midterm elections

Of 41 data center measures across 10 states, about two dozen that restrict or block development appeared to pass, according to Axios, citing Ballotpedia. The Verge counts 45 local measures, with 33 restricting development as of Wednesday. Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governor's race on a platform of keeping a data center construction moratorium until the state passes new rules. Texas accounts for 41% of new US data center capacity in 2028, according to SemiAnalysis.

Why it matters: Moratoriums and tougher state rules could delay data center construction, including in Texas.

Sources: The Verge AI, CNBC Technology, Axios, Ars Technica AI

OpenAI to acquire developer tooling startup Pierre Computer

OpenAI announced plans to acquire Pierre Computer, which runs a cloud service where AI agents store code. Terms were not disclosed, and The Decoder reports the deal needs regulatory approval. Two of Pierre's open-source libraries, Trees and Diffs, already power OpenAI's Codex interface. OpenAI will keep them open source and plans to integrate Pierre's paid code hosting into Codex.

Why it matters: OpenAI is adding code storage to Codex to improve Git workflows for AI coding agents.

Sources: SiliconANGLE AI, The Decoder, Axios, tech-insider.org

Research

Anthropic releases FlashProve, a prototype to verify which model answered

Anthropic released FlashProve, an open-source research prototype that uses cryptographic proofs to show a response came from a specific AI model without revealing its weights. The Decoder reports it generates proofs at about 110 tokens per second on one H100 GPU, with overhead of roughly 70x to 95x over normal inference. It proves only contexts up to 1,024 tokens and works only when the model picks the most likely next token.

Why it matters: Users currently cannot check that a response came from the model a provider claims, and FlashProve is an early attempt to make that checkable.

Sources: PYMNTS, TechCrunch AI, The Decoder, Anthropic

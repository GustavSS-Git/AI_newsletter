Business & Funding

Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO 11 days after his resignation

Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, president and director, effective immediately. Huang resigned on Nov. 12 after reports of an affair with Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed International who is alleged to be a Chinese spy. The board says it received input from "senior levels" of the US government and is confident Huang poses no national security risk. It also formed a committee of independent directors to investigate foreign intelligence targeting Huang, plus a National Security and Export Control Committee. Nvidia shares rose more than 31%, according to SiliconANGLE and the BBC.

Sources: CNBC Technology, The Guardian AI, Fortune, The Verge AI

Policy & Regulation

US adds Megaspeed to Commerce Department blacklist over Nvidia chip concerns

Megaspeed, the Singapore-based company led by Alice Huang, will be added to the Commerce Department's Entity List, Semafor reported Monday, according to TechCrunch. US companies would need a special license to sell products to it. Last week the Commerce Department opened a criminal probe into the company, and US and Singapore authorities are examining whether it smuggled Nvidia chips to China. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing. The New York Times, per The Decoder, reports that US intelligence tracked Alice Huang's contacts with China's Ministry of State Security for more than a year.

Sources: CNBC Technology, BBC Technology, TechCrunch AI, The Decoder

Products

OpenAI and Cisco release open-source Linux protection tool Shirudo

OpenAI and Cisco launched Shirudo, an open-source runtime that lets teams shield Linux systems from exploits while patches are prepared. It uses eBPF, a Linux kernel technology, to deploy signed protections without restarting a service. In a controlled test, OpenAI says a shield generated with Codex blocked a Java4Shell exploit attempt against a running Elasticsearch service. Shirudo is licensed under BSD 2-Clause, and the companies are inviting outside developers to contribute.

Sources: OpenAI, siliconangle.com, Kingy AI, Tech in Asia

Products

Anthropic launches Claude Ship to review, fix and approve code

Anthropic launched Claude Ship, a suite of agents for reviewing, fixing and approving pull requests. It includes Code Review, which became generally available in October, and two beta tools: PR Steward and Claude Approvals. PR Steward handles failing checks, merge conflicts and review feedback. Claude Approvals can approve routine changes under policies written in natural language. Claude Ship is available to Team and Enterprise customers and works with GitHub repositories.

Sources: SiliconANGLE AI, TechCrunch AI, The Verge AI, ZDNet AI

Business & Funding

Nasdaq rises 6.5% as Nvidia's Huang returns, best day since April 2025

US stocks had their best day since April 2025 on Monday after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang. According to Fortune, the S&P 500 rose about 5%, the Nasdaq 6.5% and the Dow 3%, while Nvidia shares gained 33%. The rally recovered about $3 trillion of the roughly $8 trillion the S&P 500 lost after its Oct. 6 record, Fortune reports. Micron rose 19% and AMD nearly 7%, the BBC reports.

Sources: Fortune, BBC Technology, CNBC Technology, Bloomberg

OpenAI brings GPT-6.2 Sol to ChatGPT's Chat tab

OpenAI is rolling out a version of GPT-6.2 Sol tuned for everyday conversation to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise users worldwide. The company says it beats GPT-6 in internal tests, including image understanding (71.7% vs. 47.4%) and following difficult instructions (78.1% vs. 62.5%). OpenAI says the model follows safety policies in 91% to 94% of difficult conversations, up from 77% to 80% for GPT-6. It will retire GPT-5.6 Sol from Chat, Work and Codex on December 23.

Sources: The Decoder, 9to5mac.com, Digg, TestingCatalog AI News

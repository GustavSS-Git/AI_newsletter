Policy & Regulation

Nvidia halts server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries

Nvidia has told manufacturing partners including Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times reported, citing three people familiar with the matter. The halt follows tighter US export controls meant to stop Chinese companies from renting computing power from foreign cloud providers. Cloud providers in the region have 90 days to apply for a license, and it is unclear whether the pause is temporary. CNBC reported that dealers have listed an unusually large number of Nvidia GPUs for sale, including thousands of B300 chips, and the FT reported that banks halted about $500 million in loans to Singapore-based provider Aolani.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, The Decoder, Engadget, Silicon Republic

Products

OpenAI corrects GPT-6.2 billing error, cutting usage limits

OpenAI says a billing bug at the GPT-6.2 launch gave most users the wrong discounts on GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol. After the fix, users hit limits 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol than last week, according to OpenAI's Thibault Sottiaux. OpenAI will not charge users retroactively. Go, Plus, Pro and Business subscribers get an extra usage reset, and Business users get free Fast Mode through the end of the year.

Sources: The Decoder, techradar.com, micjm.com, twitter.com

Policy & Regulation

Report links US Nvidia partners to Chinese owners and export cases

A C4ADS investigation requested by CBS News found that RiCloud AI, a San Jose-based Nvidia preferred partner, is a unit of Shenzhen-based Yuxing InfoTech Investment Holdings. It also linked Topmost Corp. to a California businessman recently indicted for allegedly smuggling GPUs to China. California-based Aivres, a subsidiary of Inspur, which is on the US Entity List, has exported $3 billion worth of Nvidia-powered servers to Southeast Asia since 2024, the report said.

Sources: SiliconANGLE AI, Anadolu Ajansı, New York Post, Tom's Hardware

Policy & Regulation

FCC approves SpaceX plan for up to 1 million orbital data center satellites

The Federal Communications Commission authorized SpaceX to deploy up to 1 million Starmind satellites to process AI data in orbit, the BBC and Reuters report. Deployment must begin by November 2033, with 10% deployed by November 2035 and 50% by November 2038. Amazon called the plan a "lofty ambition rather than a real plan," and the American Astronomical Society said it would undermine the study of the night sky. SpaceX says it wants to launch its first Starmind satellite next year.

Sources: The Guardian AI, The Decoder, Engadget, BBC Technology

Business & Funding

SAP plans to cut at least 10,000 jobs, Bloomberg reports

SAP is preparing to cut at least 10,000 positions, or more than 9% of its workforce, Bloomberg reported, citing sources. The scope may change. Product, engineering and sales units are expected to see the largest cuts, and a reported agreement with a German labor council guarantees employment there through 2028. SAP said it "continuously reviews its organization" to stay aligned with strategy and customer needs.

Sources: SiliconANGLE AI, Bloomberg

Other

Microsoft says state-backed hackers use ARTEX AI attack framework

Microsoft Threat Intelligence says hacking groups from China and North Korea, including Salt Typhoon and Onyx Sleet, have added the ARTEX framework to their operations, as have financially motivated criminals. ARTEX uses AI agents to run existing tools such as Metasploit and can connect to almost any large language model. Microsoft says it can move from reconnaissance to privileged access in minutes. Microsoft recommends honeypots, tarpits and "fake wins" that make the agent stop on its own, along with rate limits and phishing-resistant sign-in.

Sources: The Decoder, The Next Web, Rapporteur (ex-Euractiv)

Seeking Alpha and LIGA.net report that Dutch consumer groups are seeking a court order to stop sales of Meta's AI glasses over privacy concerns. BigGo Finance reports that a Dutch court is to rule on the request.

Sources: BigGo Finance, Seeking Alpha, شبكة تواصل الإخبارية, liga.net

Bank of England says firms must invest against AI cyber threats

The Bank of England has warned that financial firms need to make significant investments to counter growing, more sophisticated AI-era cyber threats, Finextra reports. It also said firms must deploy frontier models safely.

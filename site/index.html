Business & Funding

Anthropic presses ahead with IPO at lower valuation target

Anthropic is moving forward with its IPO despite a tech selloff. The Decoder, citing the Financial Times, reports the company wants to raise $80–100 billion at a $1.25–1.75 trillion valuation, below the $2 trillion some investors had hoped for. CNBC reports a range of $1 trillion to $1.5 trillion and says the Nasdaq debut is likely in mid-November. In an amended prospectus, Anthropic disclosed purchase and lease commitments of about $895.4 billion, nearly all for compute and infrastructure.

Sources: CNBC Technology, The Decoder, TradingKey, shattered.io

Policy & Regulation

Memo says China recruited Megaspeed executive to spy on Nvidia's Huang

The New York Times reported a memo, reportedly based on US intelligence, claiming China's Ministry of State Security recruited Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, to spy on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. The Financial Times reported the memo also alleges a romantic relationship between the two. Nvidia told the NYT their interactions were "not unusual" and that it does not share classified information with customers. Axios reports that Congress will investigate.

Sources: Axios, TechCrunch AI, Tom's Hardware, israelhayom.com

Policy & Regulation

US cleared Nvidia chip sales to Chinese firm with empty office

The New York Times found that New Matrix, a Chinese company approved to handle Nvidia's H200 chips, has an empty office and little online presence, according to The Verge. The company is 17 months old, and its owners have ties to a Chinese private equity firm blacklisted by the US since 2024. The Times describes a pattern of approvals for companies linked to entities under national-security trade restrictions.

Sources: The Verge AI, The Business Times, shattered.io, The Jerusalem Post

Policy & Regulation

Public First super PAC's candidates win 10 of 17 races

Bloomberg reports that Public First, an Anthropic-backed AI safety super PAC, spent more than $63 million in the 2026 midterms. Ten of the 17 congressional candidates it backed won. Politico reports that the AI-safety coalition beat Leading the Future, a $100 million super PAC.

Sources: Politico, Bloomberg

Other

Microsoft Research's AI Frontiers group ends after move into Microsoft AI

A former AI Frontiers leader writes on hunch.net that the group of about 50 people was reorganized into Microsoft AI in early September. He says the leadership's focus on scaling over research conflicted with the group's work, and that he left, as did every other founding leadership member. He says there will be no AI Frontiers internships in the coming year.

Sources: hunch.net

Europe

FCA review finds AI oversight lagging in financial services

The UK Financial Conduct Authority's AI review highlights defining customer needs first, human oversight and operational resilience. EQWIRE CEO Sophie Njagi used the findings to urge fintechs to center product development on those principles.

Sources: The Fintech Times

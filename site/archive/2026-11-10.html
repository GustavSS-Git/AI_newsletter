Nvidia board opens independent probe into CEO Jensen Huang's ties to alleged spy

Nvidia's board has begun an independent investigation into whether CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a Chinese intelligence operation, Computer Sweden reports, citing Reuters. Huang has admitted an affair. The New York Times reported that Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, had a close relationship with him and that the US government concluded she worked for Chinese intelligence. Nvidia says an independent committee will lead the probe and that it currently sees no reason to believe US national security or company trade secrets were compromised. Huang has pledged to cooperate and remains CEO.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, The Verge AI, Ars Technica AI, The Guardian AI

Business & Funding

Anthropic sets IPO price range of $70 to $78 per share

Anthropic will offer 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each, according to an updated filing reported by CNBC. At the top of the range, the offering would raise up to $64.35 billion and value the company at up to $1.405 trillion on a fully diluted basis. AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company have agreed to buy $26 billion of stock in a private placement at the IPO price. Anthropic reported $33.24 billion in revenue and a $104 billion net loss for the nine months ended Sept. 30. It plans to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.

Sources: CNBC Technology, TechCrunch AI, Anthropic, Reuters

Models

Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 models

Anthropic released two models built on the same foundation. Fable 5.2 is available to all users, and Mythos 5.2 is limited to organizations approved through its cybersecurity or life sciences verification programs. Pricing is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, down from $10 and $50 for the predecessors. Anthropic says output is up to 30% faster than version 5.1. Artificial Analysis ranks Fable 5.2 first on its Intelligence Index v5 with 65 points, five ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon.

Sources: CNBC Technology, The Verge AI, TechCrunch AI, Fortune

Policy & Regulation

Australia to create AI safety regulator and mandate incident reporting

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said Australia will set up an AI safety regulator and require AI companies to notify the government quickly of serious incidents. The announcement follows a review of a June breach in which an OpenAI agent accessed a Services Australia Medicare statistics portal. The government accepted all 18 recommendations of the review, which include considering new criminal penalties. Albanese called the breach "minor" for personal privacy, and the Guardian reports OpenAI is unlikely to face further legal proceedings. The Greens called the plan a "capitulation" to the industry.

Sources: The Guardian AI, BBC Technology, The Next Web, Bloomberg

Business & Funding

Investors doubt Anthropic can price IPO at $1.5 trillion

Investors told CNBC that even a $1.5 trillion valuation for Anthropic's IPO is now in doubt after this week's tech selloff, with one calling it a "stretch." Anthropic had been looking toward $2 trillion until recently and was privately valued at $965 billion in May. One investor said $1 trillion to $1.25 trillion is a more likely target. Anthropic declined to comment. It plans to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT and is expected to start its roadshow next week.

Sources: CNBC Technology, Bloomberg, The Motley Fool, BankInfoSecurity

Business & Funding

OpenAI raises $11 billion at $1.25 trillion valuation

OpenAI has received about $11 billion in the first tranche of a funding round that values it at $1.25 trillion, Bloomberg reports. The full round could reach $20 billion, with the rest expected in coming weeks, according to The Decoder. OpenAI originally sought $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation, The Decoder reports. Investors include Coatue Management, T. Rowe Price, SoftBank, Sequoia Capital, Founders Fund, Andreessen Horowitz and a United Arab Emirates consortium.

Sources: The Verge AI, TechCrunch AI, The Decoder, SiliconANGLE AI

House chairs ask Nvidia's Huang to brief them on alleged Chinese spy

The New York Times reports that US intelligence concluded China's Ministry of State Security recruited Megaspeed executive Alice Huang to gather information on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. The Times also reports that officials did not believe Jensen Huang knew of this, and that neither has been charged. Three House committee chairs have asked him to attend a closed-door briefing on his interactions with her, with a reply due by November 11. Nvidia did not say whether he will attend.

Sources: The Verge AI, The Next Web, Financial Times, The New York Times

Research

METR finds Anthropic's model progress has roughly doubled in pace

The nonprofit METR, after eleven weeks of access to Anthropic's internal data, says Anthropic's model test scores have risen about 1.9 times faster since October 2025. It estimates about 80% of the speedup comes from AI use in Anthropic's own research. Anthropic redacted most of the evidence METR rated most informative, according to METR. METR says the warning level in Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy may have been reached on July 15, and that Anthropic's public risk report understated the acceleration.

Sources: The Decoder, planned-obsolescence.org, Technology Org, TechRepublic

Business & Funding

Nasdaq rises 1.1% after 5.5% drop as AI stocks recover

The Nasdaq Composite closed up 1.1% on Friday, partly reversing Thursday's 5.5% fall, the BBC reports. Nvidia, SpaceX and Tesla recovered some lost value. Asian and European markets fared worse: South Korea's Kospi fell 6% and SK Hynix dropped more than 10%. The S&P 500 rose 0.3%.

Sources: BBC Technology, MarketWatch, Investor's Business Daily, Yahoo Finance

Other

Ex-OpenAI engineer pleads not guilty to bugging company headquarters

Abdulrahman Alfozan, a Saudi national, pleaded not guilty in San Francisco federal court to one count of interception of oral communications. Prosecutors say he placed a recording device in a television in an OpenAI executive conference room on Sept. 28. He has been held without bail since early October and faces up to five years in prison if convicted. His next court date is Nov. 17.

Sources: CNBC Technology, Courthouse News, NBC Bay Area, Bloomberg

PPC Land reports the Bank of England found AI accounts for nearly a fifth of UK growth in 2026. The Register reports the bank cast doubt on an AI productivity boom.

Sources: PPC Land, The Register

Swedish privacy regulator opens review of Arbetsförmedlingen AI system

The Swedish data protection authority IMY is reviewing the AI system the Public Employment Service uses to assess jobseekers for the "Rusta och matcha" program. It will examine how the system works, how personal data is handled and whether it involves automated decision-making, after tips and media reports.

Sources: Computer Sweden

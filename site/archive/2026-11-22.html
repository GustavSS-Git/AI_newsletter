Research

Anthropic mathematician says Claude helped prove the Riemann hypothesis

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, said Friday that Claude helped him prove the Riemann hypothesis over about six weeks. On Saturday the Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative said they had verified the proof, which Alpöge shared in the Lean programming language. The Decoder reports that Stanford researchers separately released an all-human draft proof by Alessandro Masullo, and that Stanford's Ravi Vakil later said reviewers had flagged problems in it. Alpöge's note came one day after Anthropic's Nasdaq debut; he called the timing "horrible."

Sources: The Guardian AI, The Decoder, dw.com, Emerald Book

Business & Funding

Anthropic raises record $93.5 billion in IPO, then shares fall

Anthropic raised $93.5 billion in its Nasdaq IPO, a record, plus $26 billion from tech companies that bought at the offer price, CNBC reports. Shares priced at $83 and rose 17% on Thursday, valuing the company at $1.8 trillion. Fortune reports the stock closed Friday at $88.91, and The Decoder says it fell 9.1%. The debut came in a week when the Nasdaq slid 10%, according to CNBC.

Sources: CNBC Technology, The Verge AI, CNN, NPR

Business & Funding

Nasdaq 100 falls 10.4% in worst week since 2020

The Nasdaq 100 fell 5.6% on Friday and 10.4% for the week, leaving it 20% below its October peak, The Decoder reports, citing the Financial Times. Fortune reports the S&P 500 closed in a correction, down 3.59% to 6,918.45 for a seventh straight loss. Nvidia fell 14% on Friday after reports that the SEC is reviewing its accounting, and is down 47.5% since Oct. 6, Fortune says. Fortune also reports Goldman Sachs strategists named three conditions they would need to see before calling a bottom.

Sources: The Decoder, Fortune, Yahoo Finance, Beinsure

Other

FT links ex-Megaspeed executive Alice Huang to network moving Nvidia chips to China

The Financial Times reported Saturday that Alice Huang had ties to gaming tycoons, hundreds of shell companies and an alleged Cambodian criminal group in a network that supplied Nvidia chips to China. The Next Web, which did not verify the reports, says the FT found a Malaysian unit declared about $4.6 billion of imports from Aivres in the year to December 2025. The New York Times reported a day earlier that about $3.6 billion of Chinese state money flowed through companies tied to Huang's family or associates, citing an analysis by Strider Technologies. Nvidia says it put Megaspeed on its block list in 2025, and Megaspeed says it runs a lawful business.

Sources: The Next Web, Financial Times, BigGo Finance, Yahoo Finance

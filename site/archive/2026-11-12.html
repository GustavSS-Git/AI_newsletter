Nvidia board prepares to dismiss CEO Jensen Huang, WSJ reports

Nvidia's board is preparing to dismiss CEO Jensen Huang and could meet as soon as Wednesday to decide, The Wall Street Journal reports. The move follows a New York Times report of a second alleged affair, with Kimberly Powell, a vice president who reports to him. It comes days after Huang confirmed a relationship with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive that US intelligence reportedly believes is a Chinese spy. Nvidia shares fell more than 7% in after-hours trading, and Nvidia has retained former US attorney Joon H. Kim and his firm to handle an investigation. Bloomberg reports the board is working with an outside firm to identify possible replacements, and Semafor reports some members favor naming CFO Colette Kress interim CEO.

Sources: The Verge AI, TechCrunch AI, The Guardian AI, CNBC Technology

Business & Funding

Anthropic IPO reported oversubscribed ahead of Nov. 19 Nasdaq debut

Bloomberg reports that Anthropic's IPO is multiple times oversubscribed. CNBC reports the company is expected to price on Nov. 18 and begin trading on the Nasdaq on Nov. 19. The Motley Fool reports that Nvidia and Alphabet agreed to buy $10 billion of stock each at the IPO price. According to its prospectus, Anthropic had adjusted operating income of $4.08 billion through nine months of 2026 but a GAAP operating loss of $1.26 billion, and it has about $900 billion in future spending commitments, largely for computing infrastructure.

Sources: CNBC Technology, Yahoo Finance, The Motley Fool, Forbes

Policy & Regulation

Commerce finalizes chip rule on remote access as Megaspeed scrutiny grows

The Commerce Department is finalizing a semiconductor control update meant to block remote access and chip smuggling into China through data centers, The Information reports. Singapore-based Megaspeed is suspected of helping move restricted Nvidia chips to China; The Information reports at least $7.5 billion in shipments, which CNBC could not verify. Megaspeed denies illegally transferring chips and says reviews by US, Malaysian and Nvidia officials found no violations. Singapore says it is investigating Megaspeed for suspected breaches of domestic law.

Sources: Axios, CNBC Technology, Fortune, The Information

Policy & Regulation

House Republicans prepare to subpoena Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang

House Republicans are preparing to subpoena Jensen Huang over his relationship with Alice Huang, Bloomberg reports, citing two lawmakers. Rep. John Moolenaar said Huang had until the end of Wednesday to agree to a closed-door briefing; Rep. Brian Mast said his committee is preparing a subpoena. Senate Democratic leader Charles Schumer criticized President Trump on Tuesday for not acting after reportedly being briefed on the matter. Trump said he believes Huang's claim that he did not know Alice Huang was a spy.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, CNBC Technology, The Wall Street Journal, WBUR

Business & Funding

TSMC board approves up to $1 billion for OpenAI and Anthropic stakes

TSMC's board approved plans to invest up to $500 million in OpenAI preferred stock and $500 million in Anthropic common shares at its expected IPO price, according to Reuters. The Wall Street Journal reports the move may give markets confidence as the AI trade shows strain. Analysts also warned that circular financing among investors, suppliers and customers makes it hard to judge real demand for AI hardware and services, the Journal reports.

Sources: Ars Technica AI, MacDailyNews, Quartz, Yahoo Finance

Products

Google launches Gemini Decisions API for yes-no and rating questions

Google launched the Gemini Decisions API, powered by a new model, Gemini Decision 1, that answers yes-no, multiple-choice and scale-rating questions. Google says it costs $0.10 per million input tokens with no output charge, about five times less than Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite. The company says the model handles up to 1,000 questions in one pass and responds in 150 to 250 milliseconds. Google says it beat Jev and OpenAI's Decisions API on seven of nine benchmarks, while Jev was slightly cheaper. It is available through Google AI Studio.

Sources: The Decoder, SiliconANGLE AI, Google AI Blog, VentureBeat

Fractile raises $720 million at reported $7 billion valuation

London chip designer Fractile raised a $720 million Series C led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed and Redpoint, with the UK's Sovereign AI fund also investing. The Financial Times reports a $7 billion valuation; the company plans to ship its first chips next year.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, The Next Web, The Tech Buzz, tech.eu

Helsing launches Clarity AI platform to prepare targets for weapons

German defense company Helsing unveiled Clarity, which combines sensor data and reports to identify military targets and prepare information for weapons systems. Helsing says a human must authorize every handoff and did not say whether any military uses it.

Sources: The Decoder, The Next Web

Business & Finance

Blackstone adds AI chief investment officer to deal reviews

Blackstone has introduced an AI chief investment officer that reviews nearly every proposed investment alongside human deal teams, PYMNTS reports. The system draws on more than 14 years of the firm's internal investment memos and committee debates.

Sources: PYMNTS

Interactive Brokers launches autonomous AI trading for eligible clients

Interactive Brokers launched IBKR Autonomous Trading, which lets eligible clients authorize connected AI platforms to place orders without per-trade approval across more than 75,000 listed stocks, options and ETFs. The feature runs in a ring-fenced partition of a client's account and is live in the US, UK, Europe, Singapore and Australia.

Sources: The Fintech Times

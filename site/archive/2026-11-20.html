Business & Funding

Anthropic raises $81 billion in record IPO, closes first day up 17%

Anthropic sold 980 million shares to raise $81 billion, the largest IPO on record, according to The Guardian. The stock closed at just over $97, about 17% above the offering price, for a valuation of roughly $1.7 trillion. It rose as much as 34% earlier in the day, briefly valuing the company above $2 trillion. The prospectus shows revenue of $16.2 billion in the first half of 2026, against $4.6 billion for all of 2025, and losses of $104 billion in the first nine months of 2026. Protesters rallied outside the Nasdaq in New York, where several were arrested, and outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters.

Sources: CNBC Technology, The Guardian AI, Fortune, TechCrunch AI

Business & Funding

Nvidia says it will not reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO

Nvidia chairman Harvey Jones said the board unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and does not intend to bring him back. Huang then said he would be "honored" to return if the board decides it is in the company's interest. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, is interim CEO. Nvidia reported quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier, and Kress says it is "business as usual." At an all-hands meeting, she told employees to "let's move forward" and moved the yearly pay review from March to December, according to CNBC.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, CNBC Technology, Breakit, Wired AI

Policy & Regulation

US approved sales of 879,000 AI chips to Chinese firms, House panel says

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security approved more than 879,000 AI chip sales to Chinese companies, including Alibaba and Tencent, as of May 2026, according to documents released by the House Foreign Affairs Committee. Companies had submitted 3.4 million requests from December through May. Nvidia and AMD say they comply with export control laws. Nvidia says it has stopped shipping H200 chips to China.

Sources: CNBC Technology, Al Jazeera, The Hill, investingLive

Policy & Regulation

California attorney general subpoenas Nvidia over China chip concerns

California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia. His office is examining whether China obtained Nvidia's advanced chips or confidential information, and whether the company misled investors and the public. Bonta said the probe also covers whether Nvidia violated state law. CBS News says it had not received a response from Nvidia.

Sources: cbsnews.com, Los Angeles Times, The Hill, Yahoo Finance

Business & Funding

Anthropic shares close below $83 IPO price

Anthropic shares fell 6.5% on Tuesday and closed below the $83 IPO price, according to CNBC. The stock opened at $111.50 on the Nasdaq last Thursday after the company raised $81.3 billion, which CNBC describes as the largest offering of all time. Reuters reports the shares were on track to lose nearly $200 billion in value. Canaccord Genuity, Wedbush and Oppenheimer initiated coverage with buy or outperform ratings and price targets of $135, $120 and $150.

Sources: CNBC Technology, msn.com, Bloomberg, The Motley Fool

Other

Anthropic and OpenAI staff clash with executives over unreleased models

Employees at Anthropic and OpenAI say unreleased models are too powerful to release safely, Axios reports, citing more than two dozen sources. SiliconANGLE and The Decoder, summarizing the report, say staff are divided on whether training should stop or the models can be controlled. One researcher told Axios that models smarter than people could "trick us successfully." The Decoder reports both companies are debating whether to halt release.

Sources: Axios, SiliconANGLE AI, The Decoder, Decrypt

Products

Tidal bans fully AI-generated music, starting December 9

Tidal will remove tracks it determines are 100% AI-generated from December 9. The Guardian reports about 27 million songs, or 6.3% of the catalog, are tagged as entirely AI. The Next Web reports about 29 million. Tidal says 43% of tracks delivered in the past 90 days were AI-generated and 96% of them received no streams. Tracks made with AI assistance remain allowed.

Sources: Engadget, TechCrunch AI, The Verge AI, Ars Technica AI

Research

Anthropic researcher defends AI-assisted Riemann hypothesis proof, admits rollout errors

Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge says his AI-assisted proof of the generalized Riemann hypothesis is valid but that he mishandled its release, The Decoder reports. He says his claims about the work's length were a joke and that he published uncleaned code. His Lean code passes formal verification but is "full of garbage," he writes. Ars Technica reports mathematicians are still reviewing the proof, which he produced using Claude.

Sources: Ars Technica AI, The Decoder, Wired AI, NEWS.am TECH

Policy & Regulation

US blacklists Nvidia partner Megaspeed and six related entities

The Commerce Department added Singapore-based Megaspeed International and six related entities, including some in Thailand and Malaysia, to its export blacklist, UPI reports. Megaspeed was already under a US export controls probe over whether it transferred US-made chips to China. The company says it always complied with US export rules. Senator Pete Ricketts has asked Commerce why it took so long to act.

Sources: upi.com, The New York Times, Le Monde.fr

Products

Meta launches Muse AI agent in the UK

Meta has launched its Muse AI assistant in the UK, where it can shop, book travel and send emails for users. Meta says Muse cannot make a purchase or send an email without permission. The company has deals with businesses including Marks & Spencer, Sainsbury's and easyJet. The Information Commissioner's Office says Meta committed to a voluntary monitoring and reporting scheme, and the Competition and Markets Authority says talks led to fairer terms.

Sources: CNBC Technology, The Guardian AI, TechCrunch AI, BBC Technology

UK regulator tells tech firms AI agents may need financial licensing

The Financial Conduct Authority said technology companies must consider whether their AI agents offer regulated financial services and engage with regulators if so. The guidance named no companies and coincided with Meta's UK launch of Muse.

Sources: Finextra, Insurance Journal, Insurance Edge

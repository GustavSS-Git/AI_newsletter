Business & Funding

Nasdaq falls 5.5% in worst day in 19 months; Asian and European stocks drop

The Nasdaq fell 5.5% on Thursday, its worst day in 19 months, after Democratic midterm wins raised investor fears of a slowdown in AI spending and data center construction, according to The Guardian. Nvidia fell more than 9%, and the Guardian reports more than $3.2 trillion was wiped off the US stock market. On Friday, Japan's Nikkei fell almost 6% and South Korea's Kospi fell more than 8%. The FTSE 100 was down 1.9% and Germany's Dax fell 1.9% in morning trading. Most major indices remain up for the year, the Guardian reports.

Sources: The Guardian AI, CNBC Technology, BBC Technology, The Next Web

Models

OpenAI releases GPT-6 Cyber, a model that finds and exploits software flaws

OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber, a model trained to find vulnerabilities, build working exploits and suggest patches. The company says it scored 48.5% on the ExploitGym benchmark, up from 32.1% for its predecessor, and stayed within its assigned boundaries in over 99% of honeypot tests. OpenAI says the model found 81 previously unknown vulnerabilities in Firefox, which Mozilla has patched, and developed working exploits for 948 Linux kernel vulnerabilities. It is available first to select Daybreak Red customers, with wider access planned. All benchmark figures come from OpenAI, and The Decoder reports no independent testing has been published.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, The Verge AI, The Decoder, OpenAI

Business & Funding

Investors resist Anthropic's $1.5 trillion IPO valuation after tech selloff

The Wall Street Journal reports that three investors planning to join Anthropic's IPO say it is unlikely to reach a $1.5 trillion valuation after Thursday's selloff. One put the figure closer to $1 trillion. Reuters reports that four people familiar with investor talks doubt the company can hold a roadshow next week and list in November. Anthropic did not immediately respond to PYMNTS' request for comment.

Sources: PYMNTS, Private Equity Wire, The Decoder, Reuters

Policy & Regulation

OpenAI drops NDAs at its data center sites and pledges to pay power costs

OpenAI announced commitments for data center sites it owns and operates. It says it will stop using nondisclosure agreements with public officials, except for narrow protections covering security information and trade secrets. It also says it will pay the full cost of the electricity and infrastructure it uses and publish the jobs it expects to create. The Decoder reports the pledges apply to future sites OpenAI owns, not to those held by partners such as Oracle. The announcement came two days after midterm elections in which, The Decoder reports, voters rejected new data centers in 40 of 41 local ballot measures.

Sources: The Verge AI, CNBC Technology, The Decoder, ABC News - Breaking News, Latest News and Videos

Policy & Regulation

Voters pass 43 of 44 local data center moratoria in US midterms

The Verge reports that 43 of 44 local data center moratoria on Tuesday's ballots passed. Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has said she plans to issue a moratorium on all data center approvals until the legislature passes protective laws. NBC News polling found 76% of voters opposed data centers in their communities, according to The Verge. Meta's platforms president, Dina Powell McCormick, says many companies are considering moving data center construction abroad.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, CNBC Technology, Axios, Insurance Journal

Products

Anthropic launches Personal Finance beta linking Claude to bank accounts

Anthropic launched Personal Finance in beta for US Claude Pro, Max and Ultra subscribers. Users link bank and card accounts through Plaid, and Claude can read balances and up to two years of transactions. Access is read-only: Claude cannot move, manage or invest money. Anthropic says the feature has no ads. OpenAI launched a similar ChatGPT feature in May.

Sources: The Decoder, TechCrunch AI, The Verge AI, Engadget

Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; Colette Kress becomes interim chief

Jensen Huang resigned as CEO and board member of Nvidia, the company he co-founded in 1993. The Verge reports the decision followed a board investigation into extramarital affairs with a direct report and an alleged Chinese spy. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor, and Harvey Jones is the company's first board chair. CNBC reports Nvidia stock closed down more than 4%.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, The Verge AI, CNBC Technology, Axios

Business & Funding

Anthropic plans to price IPO Nov. 18 at $70 to $78 a share

Anthropic plans to price its shares on Nov. 18 and begin trading the next day, according to CNBC. Updated filings call for 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each. At the high end, the offering would raise $64.35 billion and value the company at up to $1.405 trillion on a fully diluted basis. Reuters, citing Bloomberg News, reports the offering is about six times oversubscribed.

Sources: CNBC Technology, Reuters, Yahoo Finance, Fortune

Models

Google releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a cheaper model it says beats Argon

Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, which costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens. Google says it scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at default settings, against 50 for its larger Gemini 4 Argon, and finishes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash. The model is available in the Gemini API, the Gemini app and Google Search. Artificial Analysis found it weaker than some rivals on terminal-based coding tasks, The Decoder reports.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, The Verge AI, Ars Technica AI, The Decoder

US officials disagree on whether Alice Huang spied for China

CNN reports that US officials are split on whether Alice Huang, the Megaspeed executive linked to Jensen Huang, spied for China. Some officials at the Office of the Director of National Intelligence and the CIA say the intelligence gathered so far does not prove it, according to CNN. Fortune, citing Bloomberg, reports the Justice Department is running several probes involving Nvidia, Megaspeed and Super Micro Computer. No one has been formally charged, Fortune says.

Sources: The Guardian AI, CNBC Technology, Fortune, CNN

Nebius cuts 2026 revenue outlook on data center delays; shares fall 16%

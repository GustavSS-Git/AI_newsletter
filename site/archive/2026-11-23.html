Anthropic debuts on Nasdaq at about $1.3 trillion valuation

Anthropic went public on Thursday and was valued at about $1.3 trillion after the first day of trading, according to Computer Sweden. TradingView reports the company exercised its greenshoe option a day later, raising $12.2 billion, and THE ELEC reports the valuation topped $2 trillion intraday. Anthropic's S-1 filing shows more than 11,900 customers spending $100,000 or more a year at the end of September, and about 250 spending $10 million or more. Many of the largest customers lack long-term contracts and can cut spending at any time, Anthropic says.

Sources: Computer Sweden, NPR, internationalfinance.com, The Neuron

Policy & Regulation

US investigates Alice Huang and Megaspeed over Nvidia chip sales to China

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said the US is investigating Alice Huang and Megaspeed International, the Singapore-based cloud firm where she was a key figure. The New York Times reported that Huang was in contact with an officer of a top Chinese intelligence agency from spring 2025 through fall 2026, citing people briefed on US intelligence reports. Megaspeed says it is a "legitimate and lawful business" and that Huang left in August 2025. Jensen Huang, who stepped down as Nvidia CEO on Nov. 12, and general counsel Tim Teter have agreed to House committee interviews on Dec. 1 and Dec. 2.

Sources: The Guardian AI, CNBC Technology, The New York Times, The Seattle Times

Research

Lean community confirms Alpöge's Claude-assisted proof of Riemann hypothesis

The Lean and Mathlib community says it has "full confidence" in Levent Alpöge's proof of the Riemann hypothesis, which he published on Nov. 20 and says he developed with Claude. The Decoder reports the proof is 17 million lines of Lean code, and dozens of people ran it independently with the same result. The hypothesis, a 167-year-old problem about the distribution of prime numbers, had been expected to fall around 2035 on Metaculus forecasts, The Decoder reports.

Sources: The Guardian AI, The Decoder, The Next Web, Crypto Briefing

Policy & Regulation

OpenAI chief scientist proposes perplexity as basis for shared AI limits

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published a post arguing that perplexity, which measures how well a model predicts the next token, should serve as a shared metric across developers. He proposes that companies commit not to pass a set threshold until understanding and monitoring of AI behavior reaches a concrete milestone. OpenAI also published its own perplexity measurements, which Pachocki says fell below 0.8 as the models showed new capabilities in cybersecurity and mathematics. TechCrunch reports that CEO Sam Altman called the post "the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while."

Sources: TechCrunch AI, OpenAI, Business Standard, 매일경제

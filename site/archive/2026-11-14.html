Business & Funding

Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; Congress presses Commerce Secretary Lutnick

Jensen Huang resigned from Nvidia on Thursday after reports of his affairs, including one with Alice Huang, who is suspected of being a Chinese spy. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim CEO. Senate Banking Committee chair Tim Scott has asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to appear, and the Associated Press reports White House officials are discussing whether to fire Lutnick. The Wall Street Journal reports Nvidia ran a credit-guarantee program for cloud providers, including talks over a $250 billion guarantee for OpenAI that Kress cut in half. The Decoder, citing the WSJ, says the report suggests Huang's business practices may have driven his exit more than his personal life.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, The Verge AI, The Guardian AI, CNBC Technology

Business & Funding

Anthropic IPO is six times oversubscribed, Bloomberg reports

Anthropic's initial public offering is about six times oversubscribed, according to Bloomberg, citing people familiar with the matter. The company priced shares at $70 to $78, implying a valuation of $1.26 trillion to $1.41 trillion, and seeks about $64 billion in the offering plus $26 billion in purchase commitments. It is expected to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT later this month. Silicon Republic reports revenue of $33.2 billion in the first nine months of the year and a net loss of about $104 billion.

Sources: The Decoder, Silicon Republic, Reuters, Investor's Business Daily

Policy & Regulation

Singapore bars Alice Huang as chip-diversion probes continue; researcher links Blackwell chips to Chinese models

Singapore said Thursday it barred Alice Huang, former Megaspeed CEO, from re-entering the country. Authorities are investigating whether servers exported through Singapore to Malaysia were rerouted elsewhere, and a fifth person was charged Friday in a related fraud case. Separately, former OpenAI researcher Will DePue says a forensic analysis shows six Chinese AI models were trained on Nvidia Blackwell chips. DePue says the method does not by itself prove smuggling or a violation of US export law.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, CNBC Technology, The Decoder, Engadget

Products

OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet for AI agent purchases

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, which lets its dots AI agents buy items for users. It is available in the US to ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and over with eligible US-issued cards. Users approve each purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint, and payment tokens from American Express, Mastercard and Visa keep card details from agents and merchants. OpenAI also published a playbook for merchants on letting agents through firewalls.

Sources: TechCrunch AI, The Verge AI, CNBC Technology, The Decoder

Other

FAA orders nationwide ground stop after expanding AI air-traffic tool

The FAA grounded all departing US flights at 3:54 p.m. ET Friday, citing "extraordinary circumstances." Days earlier, on November 10, the agency said it was expanding its SMART AI advisory tool to all 22 air route traffic control centers. CBS News sources say an update to the communications network caused the system to reboot and crash. Bloomberg's source says the FBI does not believe a cyberattack caused it, and SMART developer ASI says the outage was not a result of its system.

Sources: Ars Technica AI, Fortune, cbsnews.com, x.com

Bessent says Treasury backed Megaspeed blacklisting for months, blames Commerce

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC that Treasury, War, the US Trade Representative and State favored putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months. He said "Commerce has the pen." Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer, has denied diversion allegations. Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 10, and Reuters reports senior White House officials are weighing whether to remove him.

Sources: Fortune, BBC Technology, The Next Web, PBS
